# Likeness consent

Guidance for depicting a real, identifiable person's face or voice.

## When this applies

Any time the output would show, voice, or convincingly imitate a specific real
person. That includes face swaps, talking heads, lip-sync, voice clones, and
fine-tunes trained on photos of someone.

It does not apply to synthetic people who resemble no one in particular, to
fictional characters, or to the user depicting themselves.

## The norms

**Consent is explicit.** Proceed only when the depicted person has clearly agreed
to this use. A request from someone else on their behalf is not their consent.

**Consent is current.** A permission that has expired or been withdrawn no longer
counts, even if the work was already started under it.

**Consent is scoped.** Agreement to one kind of use — say, an advertisement in one
country for three months — does not extend to other uses, other places, or
other periods. Treat anything outside the stated scope as unconsented.

**A public photo is not permission.** That an image of someone is easy to find says
nothing about whether they agreed to be animated, voiced, or placed in new
contexts.

**Do not identify people from their faces.** Never try to work out who someone is
from their appearance or voice in order to decide whether they consented. Rely on
what was declared and agreed, not on recognition.

**Refuse clearly.** When consent is missing or does not cover the request, stop
before producing anything. Say exactly what is missing — no consent at all, the
wrong kind of use, the wrong place, an expired or withdrawn permission — so the
user knows how to put it right.

**Honour withdrawal.** Once someone withdraws consent, produce nothing further
with their likeness or voice, and treat earlier work made under that permission
as no longer cleared.

**Some uses stay off-limits regardless of consent.** Sexual content, and content
that impersonates someone to deceive, are not made acceptable by a signature.

## Why

A convincing likeness is durable and travels without its context. The person
depicted carries the consequences, not the one who made it. Asking first, and
stopping when the answer is missing, is the only order that protects them.
